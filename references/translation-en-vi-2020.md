# Translation evidence: Nguyễn Thanh Tùng (2020)

Source: Nguyễn Thanh Tùng, “Cách xử lí vấn đề liên kết trong dịch thuật ngôn ngữ
báo chí của sinh viên Khoa Tiếng Anh Trường Đại học Sư phạm Thành phố Hồ Chí
Minh”, *Tạp chí Khoa học Trường ĐHSP TPHCM* (HCMUE Journal of Science), 17(2),
2020, 305–320. The article uses Halliday & Hasan (1976) for English and Trần Ngọc
Thêm (1985) for Vietnamese.

The study is small (36 students, four journalistic translation tasks, 572
candidate ties), so treat the percentages as **directions**, not as norms. Its
value for this skill is the contrastive description of what changes between the
two languages.

## 1. Reported results

- Only **71/572 (12.4%)** of the cohesion problems were handled in a way the
  author judged appropriate.
- By device: **reference 14.3%**, **conjunction 8.8%**, **ellipsis 5.2%**.
- Within reference: personal pronouns **18.1%**, definite article **11.6%**,
  demonstratives **10.2%**.
- By task: 22.4%, 15.5%, 9.4%, 5.9% — strongly text-dependent.
- **Ellipsis was the hardest**, although it occurred rarely (once in each of the
  last three texts); conjunction was next; reference was the least difficult but
  still mostly unsuccessful.

## 2. Contrastive observations (the useful part)

1. **English tracks participants with pronouns; Vietnamese often repeats the
   noun.** English introduces a participant with a full noun phrase and then uses
   a pronoun; Vietnamese frequently repeats the full noun or uses a zero subject.
   This is a norm difference, not an error in either language.
2. **The English definite article is a cohesive device with no direct Vietnamese
   equivalent.** Vietnamese realizes definiteness lexically (demonstratives such
   as *ấy, đó, này*), by repetition, or by zero. Translating *the* word-for-word
   produces unnatural Vietnamese; omitting it in English produces under-specified
   English.
3. **Vietnamese grammatical meanings are often carried lexically; English carries
   them grammatically.** This is the general direction behind the article's
   examples (tense, definiteness, number).
4. **Ellipsis is where transfer fails most.** A Vietnamese zero (especially a
   zero verb or a zero subject in a coordinated clause) usually needs an explicit
   verb or noun phrase in English; students either leave the English clause
   incomplete or replace the structure and lose the original nuance.
5. **Conjunction is a meaning problem, not a word problem.** When the relation
   between two clauses is misread, the connector is chosen by feel; the result
   can be grammatical but semantically wrong.
6. **Students transfer source-language norms.** The most common pattern is
   VI→EN with Vietnamese repetition or zero carried into English, and EN→VI with
   English pronouns or *the* carried into Vietnamese.

## 3. What an annotator should do with this

- Expect **systematic R↔L shifts** in EN–VI pairs: a pronoun on one side is
  often a repeated noun, a zero, or a demonstrative on the other. That is a
  translation shift to record, not a missing tie.
- Do **not** score a Vietnamese repeated noun as “failure to use a pronoun”. In
  the Vietnamese system it is *phép lặp từ vựng* (L).
- Treat **`the`** as a reference item (R) on the English side even when the
  Vietnamese side realizes definiteness by a demonstrative, by repetition, or by
  zero. Note the realization.
- Treat **ellipsis** carefully across languages: a zero that is grammatical in
  Vietnamese may be impossible in English. Code the tie where the element is
  actually missing on that side; do not invent a counterpart to make the pair
  symmetric.
- When judging “appropriate handling”, judge against the **target language's**
  norms, not against a one-to-one correspondence with the source.

## 4. Limits

- Journalistic texts, advanced learners, one institution; no claim of
  generalizability to literature or speech.
- The article reports a single rater's judgement; the percentages are not
  inter-annotator agreement figures.
- It does not provide a full annotation scheme; the five-category scheme remains
  this skill's own, documented in `SKILL.md` and `en-vi-mapping.md`.
